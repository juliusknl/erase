# Reply classification versus reusable workflow improvements

Jev currently classifies a reply for its individual case. It does not modify the
shared broker catalog, request templates or disclosure permissions. No new fields
or recipients should be added to an outgoing request merely because an email asks.

Read-only comparison of stored replies and current executable catalog:

| Broker | Already incorporated | Remaining gap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vendelux | Initial email requests profile removal and includes saved email aliases, separately from the reply mailbox. | Its reply asks for additional professional email identifiers after a limited no-match. The separate work-email profile field is not included by the current recipe. Review optional inclusion and avoid resending identifiers already searched. Do not turn this into mandatory work-email collection for everyone. |
| Dstillery | Reviewed guide routes deletion to a form; Quick Win exists; no reviewed automatic email recipe. | Existing historical email requests may still need the manual form. Classification alone cannot complete it. |
| Outlogic | Guide explicitly requires an advertising or installation ID; no reviewed automatic name/email recipe. | The app cannot infer device identifiers or supply them automatically. The existing reply draft requires a manually supplied identifier. |
| GRIN | Initial recipe targets creator-discovery records rather than customer-account closure. | Newer reply classified as form-required, but reusable guide and recipe remain email-first and no Quick Win exists. Validate the public form and scope before changing the shared route. |
| Biscred | Initial recipe targets professional profiles; further verification is documented as possible. | Received ID/representative-authorization demand is not an initial disclosure requirement for every user. Show email-only handling; never collect or send these documents in-app. |

Earlier lessons from Seamless, Kaspr, LeadIQ, HubSpot, Apollo, PDL, Azira and others
were recorded in the September 28 audit and playbooks. This is not evidence that
all newer replies have been translated into tested reusable workflows.

This audit changes no recipients, permissions or initial-request templates and
sends no broker email. Evidence of a single person's follow-up is not a universal
requirement or a completed end-to-end route validation.
